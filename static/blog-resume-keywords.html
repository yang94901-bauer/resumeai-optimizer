English Resume Keywords: The ATS Cheat Sheet for 2026

If your job applications keep getting radio silence, the problem might not be your experience — it might be your resume keywords.

Applicant Tracking Systems (ATS) screen out up to 75% of resumes before a human ever sees them. The #1 reason: your resume doesn't contain the keywords the recruiter and ATS are looking for.

This guide shows you exactly which keywords to use, how to find them, and how to place them for maximum ATS pass rate.

Why Keywords Matter More Than Ever

ATS software scans your resume for specific terms related to: - Job title (e.g., "Software Engineer") - Hard skills (e.g., "Python", "AWS", "SQL") - Soft skills (e.g., "cross-functional collaboration") - Industry terms (e.g., "agile", "KPI", "stakeholder management") - Education/certifications (e.g., "MBA", "PMP")

If the ATS can't match your resume to the job description, your application is automatically rejected — no matter how qualified you are.

The 3-Step Keyword Strategy

Step 1: Extract Keywords From the Job Description

Before writing your resume, copy the job posting and identify:

  1. Repeated terms — words mentioned 2+ times
  2. Skill requirements — technical and soft skills listed
  3. "Must-have" vs "nice-to-have" — prioritize must-haves
  4. Verb phrases — "managed", "developed", "led", "optimized"

Step 2: Map Keywords to Your Resume Sections

Keyword Location Where to Place
Job title keywords Professional Summary + Experience
Hard skills Skills section + Experience bullets
Soft skills Professional Summary + Experience bullets
Certifications Certifications section + Summary
Industry terms Throughout Experience bullets

Step 3: Use the Exact Match (Don't Get Creative)

ATS matches exact phrases. If the JD says "project management", write "project management" — not "overseeing projects".

Bad: "Spearheaded initiatives in cross-functional domains" Good: "Led cross-functional project management across 3 departments"

Action Verb Keywords That Boost ATS Scores

Replace weak phrases with strong action verbs:

Weak Strong
Responsible for Managed, Led, Directed
Worked on Developed, Built, Implemented
Helped with Supported, Facilitated, Streamlined
Was part of Collaborated, Contributed, Partnered
Did Delivered, Executed, Achieved

Rule: Start every bullet with a strong action verb + quantify the result.

Soft Skill Keywords (Don't Skip These)

ATS also scans for soft skills that recruiters filter on: - Leadership, team collaboration - Communication, presentation skills - Problem-solving, critical thinking - Project management, time management - Adaptability, cross-cultural communication - Attention to detail, initiative

Tip: Weave these naturally into your Professional Summary — don't just list them as standalone words.

Common Keyword Mistakes (Fix These Immediately)

  1. Keyword stuffing — Repeating keywords unnaturally gets you flagged
  2. Abbreviations without full names — Write both ("Search Engine Optimization (SEO)")
  3. Ignoring the JD — Every job is different; tailor keywords per application
  4. Outdated terms — Use current industry terms, not 2015 jargon
  5. No quantification — "Improved performance" → "Improved conversion rate by 32%"
  6. Wrong language — For international jobs, use English keywords, not direct translations of Chinese terms

The Chinese-to-English Resume Keyword Trap

If you're applying from China or a Chinese-speaking country, watch out for these translation traps:

  • "Responsible for" (负责) — Passive, overused. Use action verbs instead.
  • "Strong working ability" (工作能力强) — Meaningless to Western recruiters. Show it with numbers.
  • "Graduated with honors" (荣誉) — Fine, but include GPA if above 3.5/4.0.
  • No dates in English format — Use "Jan 2023 – Present", not "2023.1-至今".
  • Photo/age/marital status — NEVER include these on Western resumes (discrimination risk).

Final Checklist Before You Submit

  • [ ] Job title from the JD appears in your Professional Summary
  • [ ] Top 5 hard skills from the JD appear in your Skills section
  • [ ] Every experience bullet starts with an action verb
  • [ ] At least 70% of bullets include quantified results
  • [ ] Full name + abbreviation for technical terms
  • [ ] Standard section headers (Experience, Education, Skills)
  • [ ] No tables, columns, images, or graphics
  • [ ] Single-column layout, 10-12pt font, 1-2 pages
  • [ ] Saved as .docx or PDF (NOT Pages, Notepad, or .txt)
