How to Convert Your Chinese Resume to English That Passes ATS Filters

中文简历转英文简历完全指南：如何通过ATS系统筛选

Introduction

Applying for jobs in Western countries or multinational companies? Your resume is often the first thing hiring managers and ATS (Applicant Tracking Systems) see. A poorly translated Chinese resume can get your application filtered out before a human ever reads it.

In this guide, we'll cover everything you need to know to convert your Chinese resume to professional English that passes ATS filters and impresses hiring managers.

Why Most Chinese-to-English Resumes Fail

1. Chinglish and Awkward Translation

Google Translate and basic translation tools produce awkward, non-native English that immediately signals "non-native" to recruiters. Phrases like "responsible for company core product planning" sound unnatural and unprofessional.

2. Wrong Section Headers

ATS systems look for standard section headers like "Professional Experience," "Education," and "Skills." Chinese resumes often use different structures that ATS can't parse correctly.

3. Personal Information That Doesn't Belong

Chinese resumes commonly include photos, age, gender, marital status, and even ID numbers. Western recruiters don't expect this information, and including it can actually hurt your chances (and in some countries, it's illegal for employers to consider these factors).

4. Lack of Action Verbs and Quantified Achievements

Chinese resumes often describe responsibilities rather than achievements. Western resumes focus on what you accomplished, with strong action verbs and measurable results.

The 7-Step Conversion Process

Step 1: Start with a Clean Structure

Use these standard sections in this order: 1. Contact Information (name, phone, email, LinkedIn, location) 2. Professional Summary (2-3 sentences highlighting your value) 3. Professional Experience (reverse chronological) 4. Education 5. Skills 6. Certifications (if any) 7. Projects (if relevant)

Do NOT include: photo, age, gender, marital status, ID number, political affiliation, or "self-evaluation" sections.

Step 2: Write a Compelling Professional Summary

Replace the Chinese "self-evaluation" with a 2-3 sentence professional summary that highlights: - Your years of experience - Your key expertise - Your most impressive achievement - What you're looking for

Example:

"Product Manager with 5+ years of experience in SaaS and fintech. Proven track record of growing user bases from 10K to 500K+ and increasing revenue by 200%. Seeking to leverage data-driven product strategy at a fast-growing tech company."

Step 3: Translate Experience with Action Verbs

Start every bullet point with a strong action verb. Here are the most impactful ones:

Leadership: Spearheaded, Led, Directed, Managed, Founded, Orchestrated Achievement: Achieved, Delivered, Generated, Increased, Reduced, Improved Technical: Architected, Developed, Implemented, Optimized, Automated, Engineered Analysis: Analyzed, Identified, Evaluated, Researched, Modeled, Forecasted Communication: Presented, Negotiated, Collaborated, Influenced, Persuaded

Before (Chinglish):

Responsible for company core product planning and design

After (Professional):

Spearheaded end-to-end product roadmap design for flagship SaaS platform, driving 200% YoY user growth from 10K to 500K+

Step 4: Quantify Everything

Numbers make your achievements credible and memorable. Always include: - Revenue generated or increased - Users acquired or growth percentage - Time or cost saved - Team size managed - Project budgets - Performance improvements

Examples: - "Managed a team of 8 engineers delivering 15+ features per quarter" - "Reduced page load time by 40%, improving conversion rate by 15%" - "Generated $2M+ in annual revenue through new partnership development"

Step 5: Use ATS-Friendly Formatting

ATS systems struggle with fancy formatting. Follow these rules: - Use standard fonts (Arial, Calibri, Helvetica, Garamond) - Avoid tables, columns, text boxes, and images - Use standard section headers (exactly: "Professional Experience," "Education," "Skills") - Save as PDF (not Word) for submission - Keep file name professional: "FirstName-LastName-Resume.pdf" - Keep it to 1-2 pages maximum

Step 6: Optimize for Keywords

ATS systems scan for keywords matching the job description. For each application: 1. Read the job description carefully 2. Identify key skills and requirements 3. Naturally incorporate these keywords into your resume 4. Use both acronyms and full names (e.g., "Machine Learning (ML)")

Common keyword categories: - Technical skills: Python, SQL, AWS, React, Agile - Industry terms: SaaS, B2B, fintech, e-commerce - Soft skills: cross-functional collaboration, stakeholder management - Certifications: PMP, AWS Certified, CFA

Step 7: Proofread Like a Native

After translation: 1. Read it aloud — does it sound natural? 2. Check for consistent tense (past tense for previous jobs, present for current) 3. Verify all dates, company names, and numbers 4. Have a native English speaker review it if possible 5. Run it through an ATS checker (many free tools online)

Common Mistakes to Avoid

❌ Don't include a photo — Western recruiters don't expect it, and it can bias hiring decisions.

❌ Don't include personal details — age, gender, marital status, nationality, ID number.

❌ Don't use "I" or first person — Resume bullet points start with verbs, not "I."

❌ Don't translate literally — "Responsible for" is weak. Use action verbs instead.

❌ Don't include irrelevant info — Hobbies, political status, "self-evaluation" sections.

❌ Don't use fancy templates — ATS can't read tables, columns, or graphics.

❌ Don't lie or exaggerate — Be honest, but frame your experience in the best light.
